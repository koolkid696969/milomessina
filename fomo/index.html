01 — what this actually is

it's a media gig,
not a flyer gig.

Most campus ambassador programs hand you a box of stickers and a graphic to repost. This one is closer to running a small publication. You get a brand, a content playbook, and a cut of everyone you bring in. Here's the honest difference.

the usual ambassador gig

  • Hand out branded water bottles at the quad
  • Repost a graphic the marketing team wrote
  • Flat fee or just free merch
  • No real skill built, nothing to show afterwards
  • Program ends, you get a line on your résumé
  • You're a distribution channel for someone else
vs

fomo campus

  • You run an account in your own voice, about your own school
  • We give hooks and templates — the calls are yours
  • Cash bounties for completing real tasks
  • You learn content, distribution and analytics for real
  • The audience you build is yours and follows you out
  • You're building something. FOMO sponsors it.

06 — the playbook

how the good
ones do it.

Reverse-engineered from the ambassadors currently doing the best numbers. None of it is theory, and none of it requires you to be good on camera on day one.

your first week

A realistic seven days. Ship all of it and you'll be ahead of most people who start the same month as you.

day 1

Claim your handle on three platforms. Same name everywhere. Write a six-word bio.

day 2

Record your first video.

day 3

Post #1. Open the group chat. Personally invite 30 people — by DM, not by story.

day 4

Post #2. Reply to every comment within an hour. That matters more than the edit.

day 5

Put your referral link in your bio and pin it in the chat. First signups land here.

day 6

Post #3, and share it in a couple of campus group chats or class threads to reach people outside your first invites.

day 7

Check your analytics in the hub. Kill your worst format, double down on your best.

"Finance content" isn't a channel. "The money desk for Ohio State" is. Your beat is where markets meet one specific campus, and ideally nothing you post could have been posted by someone at another school.

  • Name it like a publication, not a username
  • Pick one recurring segment you can run 20 times
  • If a national account could post it, rewrite it

You're competing with everything else on the phone. The first line does most of the work, and on campus the hooks that land are almost always a number, a stake, or a named place.

  • Lead with the number: "$1,400 a semester on coffee"
  • Name the place: "outside the union right now"
  • Open a gap: "nobody at this school knows this"
  • Never open with "hey guys, so today"

Consistency beats brilliance. Five decent posts a week outperforms one perfect post a month, because the algorithm rewards frequency and your campus rewards familiarity.

  • Batch it: one shoot day, five outputs
  • Same slots every week — people learn your schedule
  • Spend 45 minutes in the comments after posting
  • Repost your best performer to the other platforms

Views don't pay — signups do. The bridge between them is always a reason to act right now: a tournament, a challenge, a walkthrough you're running in the chat tonight.

  • One clear ask per week, not per post
  • Walk people through their first trade personally
  • Your referral link goes everywhere, every time
  • Follow up with anyone who asks a question

There's no cap on ambassadors per school, and the strongest campuses run as small teams rather than solo acts. Split the beat, share the group chat, cross-post each other.

  • Bring in friends who are better on camera than you
  • Each person owns one recurring segment
  • Refer another ambassador and earn on their first 90 days
  • Campus totals combine on the national board

09 — is this for you

the honest
version.

This works really well for some people and not at all for others. We'd rather you work out which one you are now than three weeks in.

a good fit if

  • You're already the person your friends ask about money, stocks or crypto
  • You can give it 4–6 hours a week consistently, including exam weeks
  • You're comfortable posting with your face and name attached
  • You want to learn content and distribution, not just make beer money
  • You're socially connected on campus — clubs, greek life, sports, dorm life
